## Executive Summary

Stonewall Jackson Lake (2,650-acre impoundment on West Fork River) is strategically located in north-central West Virginia with exceptional access to diverse outdoor destinations. Unlike isolated destinations, this location serves as a natural hub for a network of interconnected adventures spanning state parks, wildlife management areas, historic sites, and water recreation corridors.

**Key Finding**: The 30-mile radius encompasses destinations ranging from premier state parks (Holly River, Audra) to historic Civil War sites (Bulltown Historic Area, Jackson's Mill) to unique attractions (Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum, Monongahela National Forest).

## RIVERS & PADDLING

### WEST FORK RIVER (Below Stonewall Jackson Dam)
**Distance**: 0 miles (Below dam)
**Length**: 75 miles from dam to Fairmont confluence
**Primary Towns**: Weston, Clarksburg, Shinnston, Fairmont

#### Paddling Access
- **Primary Put-in**: Tail Waters below Stonewall Jackson Lake dam (below dam access)
- **Access Points**: 26 documented access points along 75-mile section
- **Take-out Options**: Multiple towns along river corridor

#### River Characteristics
- Highly variable difficulty based on water levels and dam release schedules
- Scenic river corridor through north-central West Virginia
- Mix of Class I-II sections suitable for kayaking and canoeing

#### Safety Considerations
- **WARNING**: Low head dams throughout river system
  - Create hazardous recirculation zones
  - Risk drowning even for strong swimmers
  - Never portage around low head dams casually
- Water levels depend on Stonewall Jackson Dam releases

#### Trip Options
- Short day trips (2-4 hours)
- Multi-day expeditions to Fairmont
- Beginner-friendly sections (below dam area)
- Intermediate/advanced sections downstream

#### Outfitter Network
- Multiple commercial outfitters based in Clarksburg/Weston area
- Rental equipment readily available
- Guided trips offered seasonally

#### Historical Note
- Critical transportation corridor historically
- Multiple historic sites along river (Civil War fords, old mills)

## HISTORIC SITES & CULTURAL ATTRACTIONS

### BULLTOWN HISTORIC AREA (at Burnsville Lake)
**Distance**: 15-18 miles northeast
**County**: Braxton County
**National Register**: Listed 1984 (Civil War fortifications)

#### Historical Significance
- **Battle of Bulltown**: October 13, 1863 (Civil War engagement)
- **Union Fortifications**: Well-preserved entrenchments and ramparts
- **Confederate Burial Site**: Seven unknown Confederate soldiers interred
- **Turnpike Road**: 10-mile section of Weston & Gauley Bridge Turnpike (National Register 1995)
  - Important commercial transportation corridor pre-Civil War
  - Strategic military route during conflict

#### Structures & Features
- **Cunningham House & Outbuildings**: 19th-century farmstead (original buildings)
- **St. Michael's Roman Catholic Church**: Relocated to site (19th-century architecture)
- **Interpretive Center**: Exhibits on Civil War history, battle context, civilian experience
- **Self-Guided Walking Tour**: Accessible paths with signage

#### Activities & Experience
- Educational touring (1-2 hours typical visit)
- Photography (scenic forest setting)
- Picnicking nearby
- Park integration (fishing/boating Burnsville Lake)

#### Contact & Access
- **Interpretive Center Phone**: (304) 452-8170
- **Access**: I-79 Exit 67, north on Route 19 approximately 13 miles
- **Parking**: Available at interpretive center
- **Hours**: Seasonal (typically 9 AM - 5 PM)

#### Cross-Linking Potential
- Central Civil War heritage site in region

- Family-friendly educational destination

**Distance**: 8-10 miles west (Weston direction)

- **Blaker's Mill**: Historic grist mill that still grinds cornmeal and wheat flour
  - Operating demonstrations

- **McWhorter Cabin**: Replica of Stonewall Jackson's childhood home
  - Furnished with period artifacts
  - Interpretive displays

#### Activities
- Historic homestead touring (1-2 hours)
- Museum exploration
- Mill demonstrations
- Gift shop with regional products
- Picnic facilities available

#### Educational Value

### TRANS-ALLEGHENY LUNATIC ASYLUM (Weston Asylum)
**Distance**: 8-10 miles west
**County**: Lewis County
**Architecture**: Historic Gothic Revival (1864-1994)

#### Historical Context
- **Operation**: Opened 1864, operated 130+ years
- **Civil War Connection**: Construction delayed by Civil War; gold seized from Exchange Bank (1861)
  - Union troops from Seventh Ohio Infantry entered Weston
  - Approximately $30,000 in gold held in bank for asylum construction
  - Seized on June 30, 1861

#### Current Experience
- **Guided Tours**: Professional historical interpretation
- **Architecture**: Exceptional Gothic Revival design (National Register landmark)
- **Exhibits**: Psychiatric history, patient stories, medical development
- **Restoration**: Ongoing preservation of buildings

#### Attractions & Features
- Hour-long guided tours (professional staff)
- Photography opportunities (exterior and approved interiors)
- Gift shop
- Educational programming

#### Visitor Experience
- Fascinating but sobering history
- Excellent interpretation
- Family-friendly tours (some topics may not suit young children)
- Popular regional attraction (book ahead)

#### Access & Distance from Lake
- Located in Weston, WV
- 8-10 miles from Stonewall Jackson Lake State Park
- Adjacent to other Weston attractions (Jackson's Mill, downtown historic district)
- Parking available on-site

#### Cross-Linking Value

## REGIONAL CONTEXT

### Geographic Overview
Stonewall Jackson Lake sits at the intersection of multiple regional corridors:

1. **I-79 Corridor**: Runs north-south through area
   - Major transportation artery
   - Connects to Ohio, Pennsylvania (north)
   - Connects to southern WV (south)
   - Exit 91 provides direct lake access

2. **Lewis County Hub**: Geographic center for outdoor tourism
   - State parks cluster (Holly River NE, Audra NE)
   - Historic sites cluster (Jackson's Mill, Trans-Allegheny, Bulltown)
   - Multiple lakes within drive time (Burnsville, Tygart, Sutton)

3. **Cultural Corridor**:
   - Jackson heritage sites (Jackson's Mill, birthplace)
   - Civil War history (Bulltown, battle sites, museums)
   - Industrial/social history (Trans-Allegheny, WV history)

### Seasonal Patterns

**Spring (April-May)**
- Wildflower blooms (especially Holly River)
- Water levels high (good paddling)
- Fishing season opens
- Trails muddy after rain

**Summer (June-August)**
- Peak visitor season
- Warmest water for swimming
- Camping full capacity weekends
- Insects peak (bug spray recommended)
- Crowds highest

**Fall (September-October)**
- Foliage spectacular (especially Holly River)
- Fishing excellent
- Weather ideal for hiking
- Crowds moderate
- Water level drops slightly

**Winter (November-March)**
- Fewer visitors
- Some trails icy/muddy
- Lake levels low (Stonewall Jackson drops 27 feet)
- Clearer views through bare forests
- Hunting season active (special caution needed)

## PRACTICAL INFORMATION

### Best Time to Visit
- **Overall**: May-October (warm weather, all activities available)
- **Hiking**: April-May, September-October (temperatures moderate, foliage)
- **Fishing**: April-May, September-November (peak seasons)
- **Water Sports**: June-August (warmest water)
- **Hunting**: Fall/Winter seasons (specific dates vary)

### Gear Recommendations
- Standard hiking boots (trails vary from easy to technical)
- Life jacket required for all boat recreation
- Sun protection (water reflects sun intensely)
- Insect repellent (spring-fall, especially around water)
- Fishing license required (WV DNR - available online or at marinas)
- Hunting license required with appropriate stamps
- Water shoes for stream crossings (Audra, other creeks)

### Safety Considerations
- Cell service LIMITED at Stonewall Jackson Lake and surrounding WMAs
- Low head dams throughout West Fork River (paddling danger)
- Weather can change rapidly (especially in forests)
- Hunting season active (bright orange recommended fall-winter)
- Wildlife: black bears possible (rare); never approach/feed

### Regulations & Permits
- **Fishing**: WV license required; slot limits apply (walleye 20-30")
- **Hunting**: Licenses and tags required; check DNR calendar for seasons
- **Boating**: Life jacket required for young children; check equipment requirements
- **Camping**: Reservations recommended weekends/holidays
- **Pets**: Generally allowed; check specific park policies

### Accessibility Notes
- Stonewall Jackson Lake: Good accessibility (paved parking, accessible facilities)
- Holly River: Moderate accessibility (developed trails, some elevation changes)
- Audra: Limited accessibility (steep terrain, creek crossings)
- Most campgrounds have accessible facilities (check in advance)
