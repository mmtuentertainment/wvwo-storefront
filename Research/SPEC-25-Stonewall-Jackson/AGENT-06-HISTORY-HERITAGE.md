## Dam Construction History

### Pre-Construction Era (1930s-1966)
- **Early Plans**: Flood control dam proposed in 1930s
- **Decades of Debate**: Bitter disagreement between:
  - **Pro-Dam**: Business and urban interests who supported flood control
  - **Anti-Dam**: Rural residents who opposed displacement
- **Delay**: Controversy held up construction for decades
- **Authorization**: Finally authorized in 1966 (30+ years of debate)

### Construction Period (1986-1988)
- **Start Date**: Construction began in 1986
- **Duration**: 2-year construction period
- **Completion**: Became operational in 1988
- **Concrete Volume**: 107,000 cubic yards (enough to cover a football field with a concrete block nearly five stories high)

### Dam Specifications
- **Type**: Concrete gravity dam
- **Length**: 620 feet overall
- **Height**: 95 feet maximum above stream
- **Spillway**: Uncontrolled spillway
- **Sluices**: Five sluices through dam
- **Purpose**: Flood control (Pittsburgh District, USACE)

## Community Impact & Controversy

### Displacement of Residents
- **Forced Relocation**: Lewis County residents forced to leave homes
- **Vigorously Opposed**: Dam was bitterly contested by affected residents
- **Project Area**: Entire valley flooded by dam construction
- **Lost Communities**: Historic settlements submerged by lake

### Heritage Documentation
- **Historical Study**: "An Architectural, Historical and Oral Historical Study of Natural Register-Eligible Properties in the Stonewall Jackson Lake Region, Lewis County, West Virginia"
- **Purpose**: Document heritage sites before flooding
- **Oral Histories**: Voices Oral History Archives documented dam removal (Note: This may reference a different Stonewall Jackson Dam - verify)
- **Legacy**: Controversy and mystery still surround lakes built by Army Corps

## Regional Heritage Context

### Lewis County History
- **County Seat**: Weston, WV
- **Founded**: 1816
- **Named For**: Charles Lewis (killed at Battle of Point Pleasant, 1774)
- **Economy**: Historically agricultural and timber-based
- **Modern Era**: Tourism and recreation economy centered on lake

### Stonewall Jackson Connection
- **Namesake**: Confederate General Thomas "Stonewall" Jackson
- **Birth**: Born in Lewis County, West Virginia
- **Historical Significance**: Named to honor local-born Civil War figure
- **Note**: Name carries historical weight in West Virginia heritage

### West Fork River Heritage
- **Pre-Dam**: Free-flowing tributary of Monongahela River
- **Historic Use**: Transportation, milling, agriculture
- **Flooding History**: Subject to periodic flooding (rationale for dam)
- **Post-Dam**: 26-mile lake replaced 26+ miles of river valley

## Cultural Significance Today

### Transformation of Landscape
- **From**: Rural farming communities and free-flowing river
- **To**: 2,650-acre recreational lake and resort destination
- **Economic Shift**: Agriculture → Tourism and recreation
- **New Identity**: Stonewall Resort as regional economic driver

### Living Memory
- **Timeline**: Dam completed only 38 years ago (1988)
- **First Generation**: Many displaced residents still living
- **Ongoing Dialogue**: Community continues to process displacement
- **Complexity**: Benefits of recreation vs. cost of displacement

## Lessons from Stonewall Jackson Dam

### Multi-Generational Impact
1. **Planning**: 1930s-1966 (36 years of debate)
2. **Construction**: 1986-1988 (2 years)
3. **Operation**: 1988-present (38 years and counting)
4. **Total Timeline**: 90+ years from proposal to present

### Balance of Interests
- **Flood Control**: Achieved - no major downstream flooding since 1988
- **Recreation**: Major success - resort, fishing, hunting, tourism
- **Economic Development**: Stonewall Resort as jobs/tourism driver
- **Cost**: Displacement of rural communities and loss of heritage sites
